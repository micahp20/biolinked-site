The fatigue deserves a panel

The tiredness is very likely the Retatrutide dose — but it is worth ruling out the other usual suspects rather than assuming. Afternoon crashes and needing a nap have a short list of common blood causes, and none of them are on the panel we have: ferritin and iron (low iron is the single most common cause of fatigue in women), B12 and folate, full thyroid (TSH, free T4, free T3), vitamin D, and fasting glucose + HbA1c. A GLP-1-class compound plus reduced food intake can also shift electrolytes and glucose handling. Worth a fresh full panel — ask for these by name.

What Else to Draw

  • Full thyroid — TSH, free T4, free T3. Postmenopausal thyroid drift is common and reads exactly like this.
  • B12, folate, vitamin D — all three matter more when appetite is down and total food volume drops.

What the Labs Explain
The fat-distribution pattern, and the two levers on it

High testosterone with low-for-HRT estradiol is the combination that sends fat to the abdomen rather than the hips and thighs. That is a hormone-ratio pattern, not a calories pattern — which is why diet and training can lower your overall body fat without changing where it sits.

Lever one — this protocol. Retatrutide's glucagon arm preferentially mobilises visceral and liver fat, AOD-9604 unlocks stored fat on the release side, and MOTS-C raises how efficiently it gets burned. That works on the fat directly, without touching the hormone ratio.

Lever two — your HRT provider. Bringing estradiol up toward the usual HRT target would shift deposition away from the abdomen, and reviewing the testosterone dose if it is still high on a re-test would work the same problem from the other side. Bring a fresh full panel to that appointment and ask specifically about your E2 target and a T dose review.

Nutrition
Eating When You Have No Appetite
The actual problem

Retatrutide suppresses appetite. That is the mechanism, not a side effect — and at the dose you were on it went too far. The result is that you are not eating enough, and under-eating is its own cause of fatigue. Low protein for long enough costs you muscle; low total calories costs you your afternoons. So this tab is not built around eating less. It is built around getting food in when your body isn't asking for it.

The one rule that matters

Protein first, calories second, volume last. On a suppressed appetite you get a small number of bites per day — spend them on the densest protein you can tolerate. If you hit protein and nothing else, the day was a success. A big plate of vegetables you can only half-finish is the worst possible use of a limited appetite right now.

Training 5+ days a week at your size you burn roughly 1,650–1,750 calories a day, so a 250–350 calorie deficit is all this needs. That is about half a pound a week — so the 5 lb lands around week ten, just past the end of this cycle. Anything faster than that comes out of muscle, not fat, and it is the last thing you want at 59.
Your Three Numbers
Calories — the band
1,300–1,500
Anywhere in this band is a good day. The bottom of the band is not the goal — it is the floor of the band.
Protein floor — every day
100 g
That is 0.9 g per pound of your 110 lb goal — the line below which a cut starts taking muscle. Clear it by any means. Shakes count.
Protein target — a good day
130–150 g
Where the training actually pays off and recovery keeps up with 5+ days a week. Reachable on the plan below.
Why these replaced the old numbers. The previous version of this tab ran a 1,240–1,380 calorie band with a 500–800 calorie deficit and a 180–210 g protein target. Those were built off a 150 lb bodyweight — far too steep a deficit for a 115 lb frame, and a protein number nobody eats on a suppressed appetite. The band above is a gentle cut sized to you, and the floor is a number you can actually clear.
How To Do It
Six Things That Work On A Suppressed Appetite
01 — The Biggest Lever
Drink Your Calories

Liquid protein bypasses the appetite problem almost entirely. A slowed stomach makes solid food feel like a brick; the same protein and calories in a glass go down in two minutes and don't sit. This is the single highest-return change on the page.

The full shake — ~590 cal, 63 g protein:
· 2 scoops whey protein
· 1 cup whole milk (not water — that swap alone is 150 cal and 8 g protein)
· 1 tbsp peanut butter
· 1 banana
Blend with ice. One of these clears well over half your protein floor and lands about 40% of the day's calories.
The half shake — ~270 cal, 33 g protein: 1 scoop whey + 1 cup whole milk. For the mornings nothing solid sounds possible.
Sip, don't chug
Spread one shake over 30–45 minutes if it feels heavy. Nothing about it needs to happen fast.
Keep one ready
Blend the morning shake the night before and leave it in the fridge. The decision is the hard part — remove it.
02 — Structure
Six Small Things, Not Five Plates

A plate you can't finish is discouraging. A small portion you clear is a win. Cut the portion sizes down and add a slot — same total food, none of the dread.

· On waking: coffee with a scoop of collagen or whey stirred in (~60 cal, 12 g — tastes like nothing)
· Breakfast: 2 whole eggs + 2 whites, scrambled — small pan, small plate (~175 cal, 19 g)
· Mid-morning: the half shake (~270 cal, 33 g)
· Lunch: 4 oz chicken or turkey + ½ cup rice or a small potato (~290 cal, 37 g). Your best appetite window is usually late morning to early afternoon — put the biggest solid meal there.
· Afternoon: full-fat Greek yogurt or cottage cheese, 1 cup (~220 cal, 22 g)
· Dinner: whatever you can manage — 4 oz protein + potato or rice (~300 cal, 32 g)
Clear all six and that is ~1,315 calories and ~155 g of protein — bottom of the calorie band, protein comfortably past the floor. That is the right way round for a gentle cut. On days you want the middle of the band instead, add one or two of the density swaps in the next card.
Set alarms
Hunger is not going to remind you. Three phone alarms — mid-morning, afternoon, evening — are doing the job appetite normally does.
Never skip to catch up
A missed slot is gone; do not try to make it up at dinner. Trying to eat a double portion on a slowed stomach is how you end up nauseated and eating less the next day too.
03 — Density
Same Bites, More In Them

Every one of these is a free upgrade — more protein or calories with no extra volume to get down.

· Whole milk instead of water in any shake → +150 cal, +8 g protein
· Whole eggs instead of egg whites → +55 cal each, plus the yolk nutrients
· Full-fat Greek yogurt (20 g protein a cup) or cottage cheese (25 g a cup) → the densest easy protein in the fridge
· A tablespoon of olive oil or butter on the vegetables or potato → +120 cal, changes nothing about the volume
· Collagen or whey in coffee → +10–20 g protein, no taste, no chewing
· Nut butter by the spoon → ~95 cal a tablespoon, the easiest calories in the house
Drop the volume fillers, for now
Big salads, broth soups, raw vegetable sides and diet sodas all take up stomach space and give you almost nothing back. Keep some fibre for regularity, but stop leading meals with it. They come back when your appetite does.
04 — Timing
Work With The Shot Schedule
Monday and Thursday are the hard days
Reta days. Appetite is lowest for the 24–36 hours after the shot. Plan those two days as shake days — don't schedule a big meal you'll fail to eat.
Front-load the week
Friday through Sunday your appetite is furthest from a Reta dose. That is when to get a genuinely big meal in.
The AM block, then wait
30–45 min after the morning shots before food. Coffee with collagen fits inside that window if you want something.
Dinner has a deadline
The CJC/Ipa shot needs 2+ hours of fasting before it. Eat dinner earlier rather than skipping it — if the choice is a late dinner or no dinner, eat and skip the shot. Food matters more than one dose right now.
05 — Hydration
Reta Turns Your Thirst Down Too

You will not feel thirsty even when you are dehydrated. Dehydration causes headaches, constipation, cramping and — directly relevant — fatigue. A good share of what gets blamed on a peptide is a water and electrolyte deficit.

· 2–3 L a day, on a schedule rather than on thirst. Big bottle, top it off, finish it.
· Electrolytes once a day — an LMNT packet, or a pinch of salt and lemon in water. More on training days.
· Magnesium in the evening — keeps things moving and helps sleep.
· Coffee does not count toward the total.
06 — Bad Days
When You Genuinely Cannot Eat

Some days nothing works. Here is the whole plan for those days, in order:

1. Two full shakes. That is ~1,180 cal and ~126 g protein with no chewing — inside the band, floor cleared, day salvaged.
2. Cold and bland beats warm and rich. Cold yogurt, a smoothie, cottage cheese with fruit — warm, fatty, strong-smelling food is what triggers the nausea.
3. Ginger or peppermint tea before you try to eat.
4. Walk ten minutes after eating. It genuinely helps a slowed stomach empty.
Two bad days in a row means the dose
If you cannot clear the protein floor for two days running, that is the signal to step Reta down again to 7.5u (1 mg) — message Micah. The dose is adjustable; the eating is not optional.
Keep protein even if you skip everything else
If one thing gets in on a bad day, make it protein. Carbs and fat you can catch up on; muscle you lose does not come back easily at 59.
The Rest Of It
Training, Creatine & What To Track
Keep Doing This
Heavy Resistance Training, 3–4×/Week

The training is what tells your body to keep the muscle while the fat comes off. Protein and peptides supply the material; mechanical tension is the instruction. Do not cut training volume because you are eating less — cut it because you are genuinely under-recovered, and if that is happening, eat more first.

· Compound lifts: squats, hip thrusts, rows, presses, pulldowns, lunges
· 4–8 reps at a weight that is genuinely hard by rep 6
· Add weight every 2–3 weeks when the last load starts feeling easy
· 90–120 seconds rest on the heavy sets
If the energy isn't there for a session
Do the warm-up and the first heavy set, then decide. Some days that is enough to find the session; some days it tells you honestly to go home. Both answers are fine — guessing from the couch is not.

Track This, Not That
Protein Hit, Energy, Waist
· Did I clear 100 g of protein? Yes or no, once a day. This is the only number worth logging right now.

Storage & Handling
Keep It Cold, Keep It Clean
❄ Refrigerated, Protected, Rotated
Dry vials, unmixed
Refrigerate at 36–46°F. Never freeze. Keep out of light. Sealed and dry they are stable for many months.
Mixed vials
Refrigerate immediately after mixing, and use within about 30 days. Mix one vial at a time — not all eight on day one. NAD+, AOD and CJC/Ipa each have a first vial for weeks 1–4 and a second for weeks 5–8; MOTS-C and Semax have one vial each and finish at week 4.
Mixing technique
Aim the bac water down the inside wall of the vial, not straight onto the powder, and swirl gently — never shake. Give it a few minutes to dissolve fully before the first draw.
Syringe discipline
Fresh single-use syringe every shot. Wipe the stopper and the skin with alcohol every time.
Site rotation
SubQ abdomen, four quadrants, rotating. On Monday's four-shot morning use a different quadrant for each one.

Read This First
The Fatigue — What's Happening And What We Changed

Needing a nap after lunch when that never used to happen is a real signal, and it started with Reta. It is not you being lazy and it is not something to push through. Two things drive it, and both are dose-related.

  • You are eating too little. This is the bigger of the two. Reta suppresses appetite hard, and at the top of the ladder it can suppress it past the point where you get enough food in. Under-eating causes exactly this pattern — fine in the morning, flat after lunch. Low protein specifically also starts costing you muscle.
  • The dose itself. GLP-1-class compounds cause fatigue in their own right at higher doses, independent of food intake — partly through slowed gastric emptying, partly through the shift in how the body handles glucose.
What changed
Reta comes down from 20u to 15u — 2.67 mg to 2 mg — on the same Monday and Thursday mornings. NAD+, MOTS-C and AOD-9604 go in as a dedicated energy cycle. And the Nutrition tab has been rebuilt around low appetite, because that is the half of this you can fix today.
Give it two weeks before judging
Appetite and energy lag a dose change. Two full weeks at 15u with the energy cycle running, then tell Micah honestly whether you still need the nap.
If two weeks isn't enough
Step down again to 7.5u (1 mg) and message Micah. There is nothing lost by going lower — the AOD is covering the fat-loss side.
Worth ruling out the other causes
Low iron, low thyroid, low B12 and low vitamin D all read exactly like this, and none of them are on the bloodwork we have. The Bloodwork tab lists what to ask for on a fresh panel.

Morning Block, One Shot At Night
AM — fasted, first thing
MOTS-C 10u and AOD 10u every weekday. Add NAD+ 25u (slowly) and Semax 5u on Mon/Wed/Fri. Add Reta 15u on Mon and Thu. Wait 30–45 min before eating.

Weekends off
Saturday and Sunday, nothing. The 5-on / 2-off rhythm protects receptor sensitivity and gives you two mornings back.
Time budget
Monday is the heaviest morning at five shots — about five minutes. Other mornings are two to four. The night shot is under a minute. Lay Monday's syringes out on Sunday evening.
Tip 02 — NAD+
Slow Push, Or You'll Feel It

NAD+ is the one compound on this page where technique matters more than the dose. Pushed fast it causes a warm flush across the chest, neck and face, sometimes with a slight pressure feeling. It is harmless and it passes in a few minutes — but it is entirely avoidable.

· 20–30 seconds on the plunger. Count it.
· Draw to 25 units on the 100-unit syringe — that is a quarter of the barrel.
· If the flush hits anyway, stop pushing, wait thirty seconds, then finish slower.
· It gets easier after the first two or three doses.
Mon / Wed / Fri, not daily
Three days a week is the house cadence for this dose — large doses are easier to tolerate spaced out, and it holds the level up across the week fine.
Tip 03 — AOD-9604
Shoot It, Then Move

AOD releases stored fat into circulation. Movement is what burns it. Released fat that nothing burns simply gets taken back up, which is the difference between AOD working and AOD doing nothing.

· Fasted, before training — that is the whole instruction.
· On non-training mornings, a twenty-minute walk before you eat is enough to bank the effect.
· It does not suppress appetite. Nothing about this compound makes the eating problem worse.
· No blood-sugar or IGF-1 effect — that is the point of using the fragment rather than full growth hormone.

Honest Week-By-Week
Week 1
Appetite should start coming back within a few days of the lower Reta dose — that is the first thing you will notice, and it is the most important one. NAD+ may flush the first couple of doses. Do not expect the energy to be fixed yet.
Weeks 2–3
This is when MOTS-C and NAD+ start showing up — as a floor rather than a lift. The tell is not needing the afternoon nap, not feeling stimulated. Two weeks in is the check-in point with Micah.
Weeks 4–6
Training output should be better than it was — more work in the same session, better recovery the next day. AOD's contribution shows in the waist rather than on the scale.

Take waist and photo measurements. Expect composition to have moved more than weight. Then we decide together what the next cycle looks like.
Tip 05 — Missed Doses
What To Do If You Skip One
Reta (Mon + Thu)
Miss Monday, take it within 24–48 hours. Past 48 hours, skip it and go back to the normal days — never double up.
MOTS-C / AOD (Mon–Fri AM)
Take it later the same day if you can. If the day is gone, skip and resume tomorrow. No catching up.
Semax (M/W/F)
Shift it to the next day if the morning got away from you. The BDNF effect builds slowly, so one missed dose changes nothing.
NAD+ (M/W/F)
Shift it to the next day if the morning got away from you. Do not stack two doses closer than 24 hours.
CJC/Ipa (Mon–Fri PM)
Skip it. Do not move it to the morning — the pre-sleep window is what makes it work. A skipped dose costs almost nothing.

Hold the next Retatrutide dose if any of these:

· Severe abdominal pain, especially radiating to the back — this one gets a call, not a text

Morning puffiness in the hands or face that does not clear by week three, new joint pain, or sustained tingling or numbness in the hands.

Fatigue that gets worse, not better
If the tiredness deepens on the lower dose rather than lifting, that points away from Reta and toward something on the blood panel. Get the labs.
