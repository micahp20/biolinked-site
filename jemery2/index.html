Personalized Protocol
John's Three-Block Protocol
Fat loss first and hardest, with the muscle held through it. Cognitive sits off to the side and runs whenever you want it. Sleep and stress close the run.
The goal
Fat loss is the primary goal — 220 lb toward 190. Cognitive enhancement, recovery, longevity and muscle building sit behind it, and that is the order this is sequenced in. Extremely lean and staying lean is the stated target, so the muscle-building half is not decoration — it is what makes the 30 lb come off as fat.
What you are running
Cycle 1 — Fat Loss + Muscle Building, Weeks 1–8, four compounds. Cognitive Enhancement — three compounds, as needed, no fixed weeks, available the whole time. Cycle 2 — Sleep & Stress Relief, Weeks 9–12, four compounds. Twenty vials across eleven compounds.
Normal dosing, sensible layering
You have run one to two cycles before and you inject regularly, so nothing here is scaled down for a beginner. Retatrutide is the only compound on this page with a real titration curve, so it opens at 1 mg and climbs. Retatrutide, Tesamorelin and MOTS-c all start Week 1; IGF-1 LR3 waits until Week 3.
Sleep is the weak number
5 hours, rated 3 out of 5, with stress at 4 out of 5. That is the number on this intake working hardest against the fat-loss goal. Tesamorelin works the overnight window in Cycle 1, and Cycle 2 is built for it directly. The sleep card in the Nutrition tab runs from Day 1.

Meal Structure
Intermittent Fasting · Three To Four Feedings In The Window
The window
Noon to 8 PM is the shape this protocol is built around. It keeps the fasted morning clean for MOTS-c and Retatrutide, and it closes early enough that Tesamorelin at 10 PM is genuinely 2+ hours after food — which is the one timing rule in Cycle 1 that changes whether a shot works. If you shift the window, shift both ends.
Protein per feeding is the hard part
Eight hours and 200 g means 50–60 g at every feeding. That is more per sitting than most people eat, and it is the single thing an eating window makes harder. Plan the protein first and build the meal around it — do not leave it to whatever fits.
Feed the session
Training five days a week inside a compressed window means the session should sit inside or right after the window, not before it. The meal before training and the meal after it carry the most carbs of the day — roughly 80–100 g of carbs and 50 g of protein on each side.
Break the fast properly
The first meal is a real meal, not a snack that becomes grazing. On mornings you take MOTS-c and Reta fasted, the shots go in early and the window still opens at noon — the fast is not shortened for the injections.
Whole foods, nothing eliminated
Nothing exotic and no food is off the list. Protein at every feeding, vegetables at two of them, most carbs unprocessed. That is the whole rule — the rest is portion size.

How To Run It
Three Things That Decide The Outcome
The Real Lever
Protein Is The Only Thing You Have To Defend
  • If a day destroys the plan, hit the protein number and let the rest go. That single habit is what decides whether 30 lb comes off as fat or as fat and muscle together.
  • An eight-hour window makes this the hard number, not the easy one. 200 g in three to four feedings is 50–60 g a sitting. Weigh it for the first two weeks — most people are 30–40% under when they guess.
  • A shake is a legitimate feeding on a low-appetite day. When Reta has genuinely taken the hunger away, 50 g of protein in a glass beats skipping the feeding entirely.
  • Cheap high-protein staples: eggs and egg whites, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken breast and thighs, canned tuna, 93/7 ground beef, lean ground turkey, frozen shrimp, whey. None of these require a recipe.
  • Front-load the window. 70 g in the first meal means you are a third of the way there before the afternoon has a chance to go sideways. Chasing 200 g starting at 7 PM does not work.
Sleep
Five Hours Is The Ceiling On Everything Else
  • Five hours is the lowest number on this intake and the most expensive one. Aim for 7–7.5, and treat the bedtime as the fixed point, not the wake time. Move it 30 minutes at a time and hold each step for two weeks.
  • Caffeine cutoff at 2 PM. Caffeine has a 5–6 hour half-life, so a 5 PM coffee still has a quarter of its dose working at bedtime. This is the cheapest change on this page and usually the most effective.
  • The Tesamorelin shot is already a bedtime anchor. Fasted, two hours after food, lights out within 30–60 minutes — that sequence is a sleep routine whether or not you meant it as one. Use it.
  • Screens down and lights low for the last 30 minutes. The point is a consistent signal that the day is over. Your body clock responds to that signal whether or not anything is helping it along.
  • Deep sleep is when the GH pulse happens. That is the same window Tesamorelin is amplifying in Cycle 1 and the same one DSIP targets directly in Cycle 2 — the habits and the compounds are working on the same thing. Every hour you add makes those shots worth more.
  • Stress at 4 out of 5 and sleep at 5 hours are one problem, not two. Whichever one you move first, the other follows.
Adjusting
Tune It To The Weekly Average
  • Weigh 3–4 mornings a week, same conditions — after the bathroom, before food. Use the weekly average, never a single day. Daily weight swings 3–5 lb at your size on water and food volume alone.
  • Track the lifts too, not just the scale. Weight down with the lifts holding is a successful cut. Weight down with the lifts collapsing means the deficit is too big or the protein is too low — fix those before adding anything.
  • Flat average after two full weeks? Two options, in this order: add 2,000 steps a day, or drop about 200 calories from the carb side on rest days only. Do not cut protein and do not cut fat. Only then consider stepping the Reta up.
  • Losing faster than 2 lb a week? Add 250 calories back. Faster than that is water and muscle, not fat, and it comes straight back.
  • Expect the first week to look odd. A change in carb intake and meal timing moves water weight several pounds in either direction before any fat has changed. The trend from Week 2 onward is the real signal.
  • Expect the loss to slow in Weeks 9–12. Once the Retatrutide is finished there is no appetite compound, so the rest of the 30 lb comes from the eating side alone — slower and lumpier. That is not a stall.

Dry vials
Refrigerate at 36–46°F, protected from light. Never freeze. Unmixed powder keeps for a long time, which is why the Cycle 2 and cognitive vials can simply wait until you want them.
After mixing
Refrigerate and use within about 30 days. That window is why the compounds you have several of are mixed one at a time on the schedule above rather than all at once.
Label them
Write the compound and its unit mark on every vial you mix. C1: Reta 10→20u, Tesamorelin 20u, IGF-1 LR3 10u, MOTS-c 10u. PRN: Semax 5→10u, Selank 10u, Adamax 10u — the Semax and Selank vials look identical. C2: DSIP 10u, Epitalon 12u, Pinealon 20u, Cortagen 20u.
Every shot
Fresh single-use syringe, wipe the stopper and your skin with alcohol, rotate injection sites. Abdomen is the easiest site — two inches out from the navel, working around the ring. Rotation matters from the first week, not from the first time something gets sore.
Notes & Tips
Running This Protocol
Note 01 — Injection Routine
You Already Inject — This Is The Volume Change
  • Every shot on this page is subcutaneous — into the fat layer just under the skin. Nothing here is intramuscular.
  • This is more shots per week than you have run before — 11 a week in Cycle 1 and up to 26 in Cycle 2. That makes site rotation the thing that actually matters, not technique.
  • Work a rotation, do not pick a spot. Abdomen two inches out from the navel, around the ring; then outer thigh; then the back of the upper arm. Move an inch or two every time and give any site a few days before you come back to it.
  • Batch them, do not spread them out. Draw and give the shots that share a window back to back in one sitting — different spots, same five minutes. Trying to remember three or four separate times a day is how routines fall apart.
  • Let the syringe sit out for a minute first. Cold liquid straight from the fridge stings more and is more likely to leave a mark.
  • Air bubbles are not a danger subcutaneously at these volumes, but tap them up and push them out anyway — a bubble displaces liquid, so it costs you dose accuracy.
Note 02 — The Layering
Three Compounds Open, One Waits
  • Wk 1 — Retatrutide, Tesamorelin and MOTS-c. Reta and MOTS-c go in the fasted morning window; Tesamorelin is the weeknight shot. Reta is the only one of the three with a real side-effect curve.
  • Wk 3 — add IGF-1 LR3 and open its first 10-day block. By then the other three are established, so if the training feels different you know where it came from.
  • Cycle 2 does not overlap Cycle 1. It opens in Week 9, after Cycle 1 has finished. Running Tesamorelin and the Cycle 2 bedtime pair in the same evening is the one combination this schedule is built to avoid.
  • The cognitive block, whenever you run it. Semax alone for a week, then add the Selank, then the Adamax a week after that. Three compounds, three weeks, one at a time.
Note 03 — Retatrutide
The Compound That Does The Most And Asks The Most
  • Mild nausea in the 24–48 hours after the shot is the known profile, especially in the first two weeks. It settles as tolerance builds. Lighter, lower-fat meals on Monday and Tuesday make a real difference; fat is the macro that makes it worse.
  • Monday morning, fasted, once a week. The half-life is around six days, which is what lets a single shot hold appetite down all week. It also means a dose change takes a full week to judge — do not step up mid-week because a Wednesday felt hungry.
  • You drive the ladder. 1 mg for two weeks, 1.5 mg for two, 2 mg from Week 5. Tier down to 0.75 mg (7.5u) or 0.5 mg (5u) if nausea persists or you genuinely cannot eat. Neither direction is a failure.
  • Constipation is the other common one and it is mostly a water and fiber problem. The 130–150 oz target and the vegetables in the Nutrition tab are the fix.
  • Under-eating is the real risk on an eating window. Eight hours plus a blunted appetite is how 200 g of protein quietly becomes 120 g. If food has become a chore and the lifts are dropping, that is the signal to tier down — not to enjoy the bigger deficit.
Note 04 — The Muscle Half
Tesamorelin And IGF-1 LR3 Work On Different Clocks
  • Tesamorelin is a nightly habit; IGF-1 LR3 is a block. One amplifies your own overnight GH pulse five nights a week for eight weeks; the other supplies the anabolic signal directly, for ten days at a time, around the session. They sit on top of each other rather than duplicating.
  • The Tesamorelin timing rule is the one to actually respect. Fasted, at least two hours after your last food. Carbs and insulin blunt the GH pulse this is designed to amplify. If you ate too close to bed, skip it rather than dose it — a blunted shot is a wasted shot. Closing the eating window at 8 PM is what makes a 10 PM shot work.
  • Vivid dreams and heavier sleep in the first week are normal on Tesamorelin, and usually a sign it is working on the right window. Mild tingling or puffiness in the hands is the other known one and it settles.
  • Take the IGF shot right after training, when blood flow to the worked tissue is highest. On a rest day inside a block, take it in the morning — do not skip a day to save it.
  • Do not extend an IGF block. Ten days, then off. The gap is the mechanism: continuous IGF-1 signalling is exactly the thing that stops working. There is powder left in the vial at the end of a block and that is expected.
  • Neither of these builds muscle on its own. They raise the ceiling on the training you already do five days a week.
Note 05 — Cycle 2
Consecutive Blocks, Judged Later
  • Epitalon, Pinealon and Cortagen are 20 consecutive doses each, weekends included. The block is the mechanism — do not stretch it out over more weeks and do not extend it because powder is left.
  • The bioregulators have no same-day effect at all. That is the single most common reason people abandon one. They are assessed a few weeks after the block finishes, by whether a baseline moved.
  • DSIP is the exception — it is the one compound in Cycle 2 with a same-week effect, and it works on sleep depth rather than duration. Take it 1–3 hours before bed, not as you get into bed. If 250 mcg is not deep enough after the first week, 500 mcg → 20u.
  • Mix the Pinealon one vial at a time — roughly every fifth night — and keep the other three dry in the fridge.
  • Cortagen is the only morning shot in this cycle, which makes it the easy one to forget once the routine has become an evening habit. Anchor it to something you already do at the same time every day.
Note 06 — Missed Doses
Skip It, Do Not Stack It
  • Missed the Monday Retatrutide? Take it within 48 hours, then go back to Monday the following week. Past that, skip it and resume the next Monday at the same dose, not a higher one. Never double a Reta dose.
  • Missed a Tesamorelin? Skip it. It is a five-nights-a-week compound and one gap changes nothing. Do not take it in the morning to catch up — the whole point is the overnight window.
  • Missed a day inside an IGF-1 LR3 block? Continue the count the next day and finish the tenth dose one day later. Do not take two in a day.
  • Missed a MOTS-c? Take it whenever you remember, as long as it is before mid-afternoon. Later than that, skip it — the fasted morning window is the point.
  • Missed a night inside a Cycle 2 block? Same rule — continue the count the next day and finish one day later. Twenty doses is twenty doses.
  • Missed a whole week? Pick the schedule back up where you left it — the cycle simply runs a week longer and nothing about the sequence breaks.
