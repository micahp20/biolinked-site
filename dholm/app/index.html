?Total Testosterone567 ng/dL

Total Testosterone. The whole testosterone pool — bound plus free. It sets the ceiling for everything downstream, but on its own it can read fine while the usable fraction is low, so it is always read next to Free T and SHBG.
?Free Testosterone9.9 ng/dL
ref 5.7–17.9Normal
Free Testosterone. The fraction not stuck to SHBG or albumin, and the part that can actually enter a cell and do work. This is the number that tracks with energy, libido, mood and training response.
?SHBG41 nmol/L
ref 16.5–55.9Normal
SHBG. Sex hormone-binding globulin, the protein that ferries testosterone around and keeps it inactive while bound. High SHBG strands testosterone; yours sits mid-range, which is why your free number holds up.
?Estradiol32.0 pg/mL
ref 11.3–43.2Normal
Estradiol. The main oestrogen in men, converted from testosterone by aromatase. It is needed — for bone, mood, libido and joints — and the goal is balance, not the lowest possible number. Yours is comfortably mid-range.
?DHEA-S304 µg/dL
ref 44.3–331Normal
DHEA-S. An adrenal precursor hormone that the body converts into other steroids. It declines steadily with age, so a number near the top of the range is a good sign for adrenal reserve.
?DHT71.1 ng/dL
ref 11.2–95.5Normal
DHT. Dihydrotestosterone — a more potent androgen made from testosterone by 5-alpha reductase. It drives libido and strength, and at the high end is the one associated with hair thinning and prostate growth.
?FSH<0.3 mIU/mL
ref 1.5–12.4Low
FSH. Follicle-stimulating hormone, the pituitary signal that drives sperm production. Below the detection floor here — the expected consequence of exogenous testosterone, which switches the pituitary signal off. Relevant mainly to fertility.
?LH<0.3 mIU/mL
ref 1.7–8.6Low
LH. Luteinising hormone, the pituitary signal that tells the testes to make testosterone. Also suppressed below detection, again the expected effect of exogenous testosterone. This is what HCG stands in for when it is used alongside.
?IGF-1207 ng/mL
ref 88.5–207Normal
IGF-1. Insulin-like growth factor 1 — the downstream marker of growth-hormone activity, and the practical way GH output is measured. Yours sits exactly at the top of the reference range, so it is worth tracking rather than pushing higher.
?Cortisol, AM15.2 µg/dL
ref 6.02–18.4Normal
Cortisol, AM. The main stress hormone, measured in the morning when it should be at its daily peak. Chronically high blunts recovery and drives belly fat; chronically low points at adrenal fatigue. Yours is a healthy morning value.
?PSA0.588 ng/mL
ref 0–4Optimal
PSA. Prostate-specific antigen — the routine prostate safety marker, and one worth watching on any androgen protocol. Well under the reference ceiling.

Glucose, fasting. Blood sugar after an overnight fast. In range, though the upper half of that range is where insulin resistance starts to show, so it is read alongside A1c and insulin rather than alone.
?Hemoglobin A1c5.3 %

Hemoglobin A1c. Your average blood sugar over the last three months, so it cannot be gamed by one clean day of eating. Comfortably under the pre-diabetic threshold.
?Insulin, fasting5.1 µIU/mL
ref 2.6–24.9Normal
Insulin, fasting. How hard the pancreas is working to hold that glucose number. A low fasting insulin with a normal glucose is the combination you want — it means the sugar control is cheap rather than forced.
?Triglycerides229 mg/dL
ref 0–150High
Triglycerides. Circulating fat, and the lipid marker most responsive to diet, alcohol and refined carbohydrate. Well above range and the biggest single lever on this panel — it usually moves fast once the inputs change.
?Total Cholesterol216 mg/dL
ref 0–200High
Total Cholesterol. The sum of all cholesterol fractions. Useful as a headline but not decisive on its own, since it counts both the LDL you want lower and the HDL you want higher.
?HDL Cholesterol49 mg/dL
ref >60Low
HDL Cholesterol. The fraction that carries cholesterol back out of the artery wall — the one where higher is better. Below target, which is what pushes the ratio up and compounds the triglyceride problem.
?LDL Cholesterol127 mg/dL
ref <100High
LDL Cholesterol. The fraction that deposits into the artery wall. Above target. This, the triglycerides and the low HDL are one connected picture rather than three separate findings.
?Chol / HDL Ratio4 ratio
ref <5Normal
Chol / HDL Ratio. Total cholesterol divided by HDL — a quick read on cardiovascular risk that accounts for the protective fraction. Still under the threshold, which softens the individual numbers above it.
?CRP-hs0.4 mg/L
ref <1 low riskOptimal
CRP-hs. High-sensitivity C-reactive protein, the marker of low-grade systemic inflammation that quietly compounds cardiovascular risk over decades. Yours is in the low-risk band and is the strongest number on this panel.

?AST30 U/L

?Total Protein7.4 g/dL

?Albumin5.0 g/dL

?Globulin2.4 g/dL

?Calcium10.2 mg/dL

Calcium. Blood calcium, tightly controlled by parathyroid hormone and vitamin D. Just over the ceiling; a mildly high calcium alongside a vitamin D of 66 is usually worth confirming on a repeat draw.
?Sodium139 mmol/L

Sodium. The main electrolyte governing fluid balance and nerve signalling. Mid-range and normal.
?Chloride104 mmol/L

Chloride. Sodium's partner electrolyte, also part of the acid-base picture. Normal, though it is read together with the low CO2 above.
?TSH1.59 µIU/mL

TSH. Thyroid-stimulating hormone — the pituitary's instruction to the thyroid, and the most sensitive single thyroid marker. A low-normal TSH like this suggests the thyroid is keeping up without being pushed.
?Free T34.2 pg/mL

Free T3. The active thyroid hormone that actually sets metabolic rate at the cell. Near the top of range, which fits the low-normal TSH.
