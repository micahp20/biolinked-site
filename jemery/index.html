If you shift the window, shift both ends
Noon–8 is not sacred, but the eight-hour length and the two-hour gap before the Ipamorelin shot are. An 11–7 window with a 9 PM shot works identically. A window that drifts to 2–10 PM does not — it collides with the one timing rule in Cycle 1.
Fifty grams a feeding is the hard part
Eight hours and 200 g means every single feeding carries 50 g, which is more per sitting than most people have ever eaten. Plan the protein first and build the meal around it. If a feeding lands at 25 g, the day is already lost — you cannot make it up at 7:45.
Rest days drop a feeding, not the protein
Three feedings of 60–70 g instead of four of 50 g. Same 200 g, 100 fewer grams of carbs. Do not use a rest day as a reason to also eat less protein — rest days are when the repair actually happens.
What breaks a fast and what does not
Black coffee, plain tea, water, and zero-calorie electrolytes do not. Anything with protein or calories does, including a splash of milk and including BCAAs. The one deliberate exception is below, if you train in the morning.

Sleep & Recovery
Five Hours Is The Ceiling On Everything Else
The Biggest Free Lever
Two More Hours Is Worth More Than Any Change On This Tab
  • Five hours is the lowest number on your intake and the most expensive one. On short sleep, a fixed calorie deficit takes a meaningfully larger share of its weight out of lean tissue instead of fat — which is a direct tax on “extremely lean.” Target 7–7.5 hours.
  • Move the bedtime, not the wake time. Most people at 5 hours are not waking early — they are going to bed late. Shift it 30 minutes at a time and hold each step for two weeks before deciding it did not work.
  • Caffeine cutoff at 2 PM, 400 mg ceiling. The half-life is 5–6 hours, so a 5 PM coffee still has a quarter of its dose active at bedtime. If you train at 2:30 and want a pre-workout, that is your last caffeine of the day, and it should be the only reason you are still dosing that late.
  • Evening carbs help sleep — this is the opposite of the folklore. The ~50 g of carbs in the 7:45 feeding is deliberate: carbohydrate improves tryptophan uptake into the brain, which is upstream of serotonin and melatonin. Cutting carbs at night is one of the most common self-inflicted sleep problems in a cut.
  • Magnesium glycinate, 400 mg, evening. Plus the food sources in the hydration table. Deficits and heavy training both deplete magnesium, and low magnesium shows up as difficulty staying asleep rather than falling asleep.
  • 3 g of glycine before bed is a cheap addition with a real signal for sleep onset and quality — and it is already what makes the magnesium glycinate the right form to buy.
  • Do not go to bed hungry. On Retatrutide you may not feel like the 7:45 feeding — eat it anyway. Waking at 3 AM under-fed on a 5-hour night is the worst available outcome, and the last feeding is what prevents it.
  • You already have a bedtime routine and did not ask for one. Window closes 8 PM → Ipamorelin 10 PM → lights out within 30–60 minutes. That sequence is a sleep protocol — screens down and lights low for the last 30 minutes is the only piece missing.
  • Stress at 4 out of 5 and sleep at 5 hours are one problem, not two. Whichever one you move first, the other follows.
How To Run It
Three Cards That Decide The Outcome
Card 01 — The Real Risk
A Suppressed Appetite Is How Protein Quietly Becomes 120 g
  • Eight-hour window plus blunted appetite is the exact combination that produces accidental muscle loss. Not overeating — under-eating protein on the days you are not hungry. That is the one thing on this tab that can quietly ruin the result.
  • Hit 200 g whether or not you feel like eating. If a day falls apart, hit the protein number and let the calories be sloppy. Calories can miss for a day; protein should not.
  • Front-load the window. 60 g by 12:30 PM means you are a third of the way there before the afternoon can go sideways. Chasing 200 g starting at 7 PM does not work, and it will not fit.
  • Two scoops of whey is a legitimate feeding on a genuinely low-appetite day. Liquid calories go down when solid food will not, which is a feature on Retatrutide, not a compromise.
  • Weigh your protein for two weeks. Not forever — just long enough to learn what 6 oz and 8 oz actually look like. Nearly everyone is 30–40% under when they guess, and at 50 g a feeding that error is the whole margin.
  • Keep 40 g of emergency protein in the house at all times. Canned tuna, a whey tub, pre-cooked chicken. The days you need it are the days you least want to cook.
Card 02 — Adjusting
Tune It To The Weekly Average, Never A Single Day
  • Weigh four or more mornings a week, same conditions — after the bathroom, before food, before water. Use the weekly average. Daily weight swings 3–5 lb at your size on food volume and water alone, and an eating window exaggerates that further.
  • Track the lifts alongside the scale. Weight down with the lifts holding is a successful cut. Weight down with the lifts collapsing means the deficit is too big or the protein is too low — fix those two before changing anything else.
  • Flat average after two full weeks? In this order: add 2,000 steps a day, then cut 200 calories from rest-day carbs only. Never cut protein and never cut below 70 g of fat. The Retatrutide ladder climbs on its own schedule — do not jump it ahead.
  • Losing faster than 2 lb a week? Add 250 calories back, as carbs on training days. Faster than that is water and lean tissue, and it comes straight back.
  • Expect Week 1 to look strange. Changing carb intake and meal timing at once moves water weight several pounds in either direction before any fat has changed. The trend from Week 2 onward is the real signal.
  • Expect the loss to slow after Week 8. With the Retatrutide finished there is no appetite compound, so the rest of the 30 lb comes from the eating side alone — slower and lumpier. That is not a stall and it is not a reason to add anything.
Card 03 — Staying Lean
The Half Of “Get Lean And Stay Lean” That People Skip
  • Do not jump to maintenance the day you hit 190. Going from 2,450 straight to 2,950 puts several pounds back on inside two weeks and reads as failure when it is just food volume and glycogen.
  • Add 100–150 calories a week, as carbs, for five to eight weeks until you are eating around 2,950. Weigh through it. A pound or two up across the whole reverse is normal and expected.
  • Protein never comes down. 190–200 g is the permanent number, in a deficit and at maintenance both. It is also the single most useful habit you carry into your forties — the anti-aging goal on your intake is mostly a muscle-retention goal.
  • Keep the window and keep the five sessions. Those two habits are what actually hold the result — not the specific calorie number, which changes constantly.
  • Act on a 5 lb rise, not a 2 lb one. Pick a ceiling — 195 is sensible — and when the weekly average touches it, drop back to the 195→190 numbers for two or three weeks. That is a maintenance system, and it is what “stay lean” actually means.
  • The longevity foods are already in the plan: salmon two to three times a week, cruciferous and leafy greens at three feedings, olive oil as the default fat, a cup of berries daily. Nothing extra to buy — the anti-aging half of your goal list is the same food as the fat-loss half.

Wipe both stoppers with alcohol. Let the water run slowly down the inside wall of the vial — do not squirt it onto the powder. Swirl gently until clear. Never shake.
When to mix
Only the vial you are starting. Wk 1: MOTS-c, Reta #1, NAD+ #1, Ipamorelin #1, CJC #1. Wk 3: IGF-1 LR3 #1. Wk 5: Reta #2. Wk 7: IGF-1 LR3 #2. The two cognitive vials stay dry until you decide to run them.
Storage
Dry vials refrigerated at 36–46°F, out of light, never frozen. Once mixed, use within about 30 days.
Label them
Write the compound and its unit mark on every vial you mix. Ipamorelin and CJC-1295 w/ DAC are both 3.33 mg/mL and look identical, but the draws are 10u and 30u — label those two first.
Notes & Tips
Running This Protocol
Note 01 — Injections
Batch By Window, Rotate Every Site
  • Every shot here is subcutaneous — into the fat layer under the skin. Nothing is intramuscular.
  • Batch by window, not by compound. The morning shots go back to back in one sitting, different sites, same five minutes. Same for the evening.
  • Rotate properly. Abdomen two inches out from the navel gives you eight to ten sites; add outer thighs and the backs of the arms. Give any site a few days before coming back to it.
  • Let the syringe sit out for a minute first. Cold liquid straight from the fridge stings more and is likelier to leave a mark.
  • NAD+ goes in slow — 30 to 60 seconds, not a quick push.
Note 02 — Retatrutide
The One That Titrates
  • Monday morning, fasted, once a week. The half-life is around six days, so a dose change takes a full week to judge — do not step up mid-week because a Wednesday felt hungry.
  • Mild nausea in the first 24–48 hours after the shot is the known profile and settles as tolerance builds. Lighter, lower-fat meals on Monday and Tuesday help; fat is the macro that makes it worse.
  • The ladder climbs every Monday. 1 mg (10u) in Week 1, then +1 mg a week — 2 mg, 3 mg, 4 mg, 5 mg, 6 mg, 7 mg — to 8 mg (80u) in Week 8. 8 mg is the ceiling; reevaluate there.
  • Constipation is the other common one — water and fiber fix it. The targets are in the Nutrition tab.
  • Under-eating is the real risk on an eating window. Eight hours plus a blunted appetite is how 200 g of protein quietly becomes 120 g.
Note 03 — GH Axis And IGF-1 LR3
Different Clocks
  • Ipamorelin and CJC-1295 w/ DAC are two halves of one job. The weekly CJC holds the GHRH baseline; the nightly Ipamorelin triggers the actual pulse. Neither replaces the other.
  • The evening timing rule is the one to respect. Fasted, at least two hours after your last food, then lights out within 30–60 minutes. Food blunts the pulse. If you ate too close to bed, skip it rather than dose it.
  • Vivid dreams and heavier sleep in the first week are normal, and usually a sign it is working on the right window.
  • Take the IGF shot right after training, when blood flow to the worked tissue is highest. On a rest day inside a block, take it in the morning.
  • Do not extend an IGF block. Ten days, then off — the gap is the mechanism. Powder left in the vial at the end of a block is expected.
Note 04 — Missed Doses
Skip It, Do Not Stack It
  • Missed the Monday Retatrutide? Take it within 48 hours, then go back to Monday. Past that, skip it and pick the ladder back up the next Monday at that week's dose. Never double a Reta dose.
  • Missed an Ipamorelin? Skip it — one gap in a five-nights-a-week compound changes nothing. Do not take it in the morning to catch up.
  • Missed the Sunday CJC-1295 w/ DAC? Take it within a day or two, then pick one day and stay on it.
  • Missed a day inside an IGF block? Continue the count the next day and finish one day later. Do not take two in a day.
  • Missed a MOTS-c or NAD+? Take it before mid-afternoon or skip it — the fasted morning window is the point.
