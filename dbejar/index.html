Dominic's Two-Block Protocol
Fat loss and muscle together, because that is the pair you came here for. The cognitive block sits off to the side and runs whenever you want it.
In your words
You listed fat loss as the primary goal, with muscle building, cognitive enhancement, and sleep & stress behind it. 165 lb toward 145, while training three to four times a week. That order is exactly how this is sequenced — the fat-loss and muscle work run first and together, and nothing competes with them.
You have never injected before
And nothing on this page has been in your system before either, so both facts shape every number below. Every dose here opens at the low end of its range and nothing starts on the same day as anything else. The needles are insulin syringes — shorter than a grain of rice and thinner than a hair — and they go into the fat layer under the skin, not into muscle. Week 1 has one job: learn the mechanics on two compounds. Everything else waits.
Sensible dosing, not timid dosing
You are 21, healthy, and already training — that is the easiest starting position there is, and the doses reflect it. This is not an ultra-conservative protocol; it is a normal male protocol with a two-week on-ramp in front of it. The Retatrutide ladder in particular is built to keep moving up rather than to park at the entry dose.
What you are running
Cycle 1 — Fat Loss + Muscle Building, Weeks 1–8, four compounds. Cognitive Enhancement — three compounds, as needed, no fixed weeks, available the whole time. That is the whole protocol. You decided against the Sleep & Stress cycle, so it is not scheduled, not billed and not on your calendar — it is kept at the bottom of this tab as a reference record only.
The 20 pounds
165 to 145. At the pace this is built for — 1 to 1.5 lb a week — that is roughly 14 to 20 weeks, which is longer than Cycle 1 runs. Expect to be 8 to 12 lb down by the end of Week 8 and to finish the rest on the eating side. At your age, with resistance training already in place, that rate takes it off as fat and leaves the muscle where it is.
You already train — keep training
Three to four sessions a week is the single biggest advantage on this intake, and it is what makes the muscle-building half of Cycle 1 worth running at all. Do not add cardio on top of a deficit; keep the lifting, add steps. The Nutrition tab has the shape.
Sleep is still the weak link
You rated sleep 3 out of 5 at 6.5 hours with stress at 3 out of 5. That is the one number on this intake that is quietly working against both goals — recovery, appetite regulation and training output all run through it. You passed on the cycle built for it, which is a fine call, but it means the sleep-hygiene card in the Nutrition tab is doing that job on its own. It is free, and it is worth actually doing. The CJC/Ipamorelin in Cycle 1 also works the overnight window, so Cycle 1 is not silent on this.
Your prescriber
You are under a PCP's care and taking prescription medication daily. Nothing here is a reason to change any of that, and none of it comes off. The one item worth mentioning at your next visit is in the Notes tab.

Retatrutide · 30 mg Fat Loss ?A triple agonist — GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. It slows gastric emptying, quiets appetite and improves blood-sugar handling, and the glucagon arm adds a direct push on energy expenditure and liver fat. That third arm is what separates it from the older GLP-1s, and its ~6-day half-life is what lets it run once a week. 30 mg in 3 mL is 10 mg/mL, so 10 units is exactly 1 mg.

Why some of these stop early

A mixed vial keeps about 30 days refrigerated, and that is what sets the block lengths on this page. Three things worth knowing. One: the paired compounds — Retatrutide, CJC/Ipamorelin and IGF-1 LR3 — are one vial per four-week half, which is why Cycle 1 runs eight weeks rather than four. Two: the Retatrutide vial holds far more than you will use inside 30 days, so it gets swapped on the calendar, not on what is left in it. Three: MOTS-c is counted in doses rather than weeks — 30 of them — which is why it finishes in Week 6 while the rest of the cycle carries on. Mix only the block you are in — the cognitive vials stay dry until you decide to run them.

Nutrition
Fueling 165 → 145 Without Losing The Muscle
The frame

You want two things that usually fight each other — less fat and more muscle — and at 21, already training three to four times a week, that is genuinely achievable. It takes a moderate deficit, not an aggressive one, and protein high enough that the deficit only ever comes out of fat. Three numbers decide the outcome: calories you can live at, protein you defend, and carbs placed around training. Everything below is a starting point to tune against the weekly average, not a prescription.

Meal Structure
Four Feedings · Protein At Every One
Anchor
A protein-forward breakfast, every day. It sets the day's protein total up to be hit rather than chased at 10pm, and it is the single habit that makes the rest of this easy. On mornings you take MOTS-c and Reta fasted, that means waiting 30–45 minutes after the shot, then eating properly.
Feed the session
The meal before training and the meal after it carry the most carbs of the day. Roughly 60–80 g of carbs and 40 g of protein on each side of the session. This is not a detail — it is most of the difference between training well in a deficit and grinding through it.
Protein first, then the vegetable, then the carb
Literally the order you eat the food on the plate. It blunts the glucose rise after the meal and it means you fill up on the thing you most need. Free, and it works at restaurants too.
Whole foods, nothing eliminated
Nothing exotic and no food is off the list. Protein at every feeding, vegetables at two of them, most carbs unprocessed. That is the whole rule — the rest is portion size. A plan you will still be running when the last of the 20 lb comes off beats a stricter one you abandon in Week 3.

Weeks 1–8 — eating on Retatrutide

The targets above do not change; your appetite does. Reta quiets hunger, which sounds like the whole point and is also the main way people on a GLP-1 accidentally lose muscle instead of fat. Three rules for Cycle 1. One: hit the protein number whether or not you feel like eating — a shake is a legitimate meal on a low-appetite day. Two: Reta blunts thirst as well as hunger, so add 20 oz of water on Monday and Tuesday. Three: keep Monday and Tuesday meals lighter and lower in fat — that is the 48-hour window after the weekly shot, when nausea is most likely, and fat is the macro that makes it worse. Do not train fasted on a Monday in the first two weeks.

How To Run It
Three Things That Decide The Outcome
The Real Lever
Protein Is The Only Thing You Have To Defend
  • If a day destroys the plan, hit the protein number and let the rest go. That single habit is what decides whether 20 lb comes off as fat or as fat and muscle together. Calories can be sloppy for a day; protein should not be.
  • Front-load it. 40 g at breakfast means you are already a quarter of the way there before the day has a chance to go sideways. Chasing 165 g starting at dinner does not work.
  • A shake is a legitimate meal on a low-appetite day. When Reta has genuinely taken the hunger away, 40 g of protein in a glass is far better than skipping the feeding.
  • Cheap high-protein staples: eggs and egg whites, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken thighs, canned tuna, lean ground turkey, frozen shrimp, whey. None of these require a recipe.
  • Weigh the protein for the first two weeks. Not forever — just long enough to learn what 6 oz actually looks like. Nearly everyone is 30–40% under when they guess.
Sleep
Six And A Half Hours Is The Ceiling On Everything Else
  • Aim for 7.5–8 hours, and treat the bedtime as the fixed point, not the wake time. Most people who sleep 6.5 hours are not waking early — they are going to bed late. Move it 30 minutes and hold it for two weeks before deciding it did not work.
  • Caffeine cutoff at 2pm. Caffeine has a 5–6 hour half-life, so a 5pm coffee still has a quarter of its dose working at bedtime. This is the cheapest change on this page and usually the most effective.
  • The CJC/Ipamorelin shot is already a bedtime anchor. Fasted, two hours after food, lights out within 30–60 minutes — that sequence is a sleep routine whether or not you meant it as one. Use it.
  • Screens down and lights low for the last 30 minutes. Not a lifestyle overhaul — the point is a consistent signal that the day is over. Your body clock responds to that signal whether or not anything is helping it along.
  • Deep sleep is when the GH pulse happens. That is the same window CJC/Ipamorelin is amplifying in Cycle 1 — so the sleep habits and the compound are working on the same thing, not on different ones. Every hour you add to the night makes that shot worth more.
Adjusting
Tune It To The Weekly Average
  • Weigh 3–4 mornings a week, same conditions — after the bathroom, before food. Use the weekly average, never a single day. Daily weight swings 2–4 lb on water and food volume alone.
  • Track the lifts too, not just the scale. Weight down with the lifts holding is a successful cut. Weight down with the lifts collapsing means the deficit is too big or the protein is too low — fix those before adding anything.
  • Flat average after two full weeks? Two options, in this order: add 2,000 steps a day, or drop about 150 calories from the carb side on rest days only. Do not cut protein and do not cut fat. Only then consider stepping the Reta up.
  • Losing faster than 2 lb a week? Add 200 calories back. Faster than that at your size is water and muscle, not fat, and it comes straight back.
  • Expect the first week to look odd. A change in carb intake moves water weight several pounds in either direction before any fat has changed. The trend from Week 2 onward is the real signal.
  • Expect the loss to slow after Week 8. Once the Retatrutide is finished there is no appetite compound, so the rest of the 20 lb comes from the eating side alone — slower and lumpier. That is not a stall and it is not a reason to add anything.

Cycle 1

Do not mix any of these until you actually intend to start — a mixed vial has about a 30-day window and this block has no scheduled start date.
The one small mix
Adamax takes only 1 mL — the smallest mix on the page, and the easiest to overfill on autopilot. Mixed at 2 mL out of habit, the same 10u draw would be half the intended dose. Measure it out deliberately and label it the moment it is mixed.
How
Wipe both stoppers with alcohol. Draw the water into a syringe, then let it run slowly down the inside wall of the vial — do not squirt it straight onto the powder. Swirl gently until clear. Never shake. Take your time on the first one; there is no rush and nothing is fragile except the peptide itself.
It looks like nothing
The powder is often just a faint film at the bottom of the vial — that is normal, not a short vial, and it is most obvious on the little 5 mg Adamax vial. Everything on this page goes completely clear once mixed.
Order — one block at a time
Mix only the block you are starting. Wk 1: MOTS-c and Reta #1 on Monday; CJC/Ipa #1 in Wk 2. Wk 3: IGF-1 LR3 #1 on the Monday the block opens. Wk 5: Reta #2. Wk 6: CJC/Ipa #2 — the first vial's 20 doses run out at the end of Wk 5. Wk 7: IGF-1 LR3 #2. The three cognitive vials stay dry until you actually decide to run them — they have no start date, and a mixed vial only keeps about 30 days.
Storage & Handling
Keep It Cold · Keep It Clean
❄ Refrigerated, Protected, Labeled
Dry vials
Refrigerate at 36–46°F, protected from light. Never freeze. Unmixed powder keeps for a long time, which is why the cognitive vials can simply wait until you want them.
After mixing
Refrigerate and use within about 30 days. That 30-day window is what makes four weeks the block length in this protocol, and it is why the compounds you have two of are one vial per four-week half. The Retatrutide vial holds far more than 30 days of doses at one shot a week — it gets swapped on the calendar, not on what is left in it.
Label them
Write the compound and its unit mark on every vial you mix. C1: Reta 10→20u, CJC/Ipa 10u, IGF-1 LR3 10u, MOTS-c 10u — all four are a 10u draw at the start, so the label is what tells them apart. PRN: Semax 5→10u, Selank 10u, Adamax 10u — the Semax and Selank vials look identical, and that is the pair worth labelling carefully.
Every shot
Fresh single-use syringe, wipe the stopper and your skin with alcohol, rotate injection sites. Abdomen is the easiest site — two inches out from the navel, working around the ring. Rotation matters from the first week, not from the first time something gets sore.
Notes & Tips
Running Your First Protocol
Note 01 — Your First Injection
You Have Never Done This Before — Start Here
  • Every shot on this page is subcutaneous — into the fat layer just under the skin, not into muscle. That is the easy kind. The needle is an insulin needle: shorter than a grain of rice and thinner than a hair, and most people describe the first one as anticlimactic.
  • Abdomen is the easiest site. Two inches out from the navel, anywhere around the ring. Outer thigh and the back of the upper arm also work once you are comfortable.
  • Pinch, insert at 45–90°, push slowly, withdraw. That is the whole technique. Pinching a fold of skin makes it obvious you are in the fat layer and not deeper.
  • Do the first one on a Monday morning with time to spare, not on a day you are rushing out the door. Lay everything out first: vial, syringe, two alcohol pads, sharps container.
  • Rotate. Move an inch or two from the last spot each time and work your way around the ring. Repeating the same square inch is what causes lumps and tenderness.
  • A small bruise or a red dot is normal and fades in a day or two. Injecting cold liquid straight from the fridge stings more — let the syringe sit out for a minute first.
  • Air bubbles are not a danger subcutaneously at these volumes, but tap them up and push them out anyway — a bubble displaces liquid, so it costs you dose accuracy.
  • Batch them, do not spread them out. Draw and give the shots that share a window back to back in one sitting — different spots, same five minutes. Trying to remember three separate times a day is how routines fall apart.
Note 02 — The On-Ramp
Nothing Starts On The Same Day
  • Never start more than two new compounds in the same week. Everything on this page is new to you, so this rule is what makes the whole protocol interpretable — if it all starts at once you learn nothing about any of it.
  • Wk 1 — MOTS-c and Retatrutide only. Reta is the compound with a real side-effect curve, so give it a clean week to be judged in. MOTS-c goes in the same fasted morning window and is unremarkable to take.
  • Wk 2 — add CJC/Ipamorelin. Your first evening shot, and it has a timing rule worth learning on its own: fasted, two hours after food, lights out within the hour.
  • Wk 3 — add IGF-1 LR3 and open its first 10-day block. By now the other three are established, so if the training feels different you know where it came from.
  • The cognitive block, whenever you run it. Semax alone for a week, then add the Selank, then the Adamax a week after that. Three compounds, three weeks, one at a time.
Note 03 — Retatrutide
The Compound That Does The Most And Asks The Most
  • Mild nausea in the 24–48 hours after the shot is the known profile, especially in the first two weeks. It settles as tolerance builds. Lighter, lower-fat meals on Monday and Tuesday make a real difference; fat is the macro that makes it worse.
  • Monday morning, fasted, once a week. The half-life is around six days, which is what lets a single shot hold appetite down all week. It also means a dose change takes a full week to judge — do not step up mid-week because a Wednesday felt hungry.
  • You drive the ladder. 1 mg for two weeks, 1.5 mg for two, 2 mg from Week 5 — that is the plan, and the steps are expected unless something gives you a reason to hold. Tier down to 0.75 mg (7.5u) or 0.5 mg (5u) if nausea persists or you genuinely cannot eat. Neither direction is a failure; tell Micah which way you went.
  • Constipation is the other common one and it is mostly a water and fiber problem. The 110–130 oz target and the vegetables in the Nutrition tab are the fix.
  • Under-eating is the real risk, not overeating. If food has become a chore and the lifts are dropping, that is the signal to tier down — not to enjoy the bigger deficit. Losing 20 lb at 1–1.5 a week with your strength intact is the goal; losing it faster without is not.
  • Tell your PCP you are on a GLP-1-class compound. You are under their care and it is relevant to anything else they might prescribe — particularly anything that affects blood sugar or gastric emptying, and to any procedure requiring sedation. Nothing you currently take needs to change.
Note 04 — The Muscle Half
CJC/Ipamorelin And IGF-1 LR3 Work On Different Clocks
  • CJC/Ipamorelin is a nightly habit; IGF-1 LR3 is a block. One amplifies your own overnight GH pulse five nights a week for eight weeks; the other supplies the anabolic signal directly, for ten days at a time, around the session. They are not redundant — they sit on top of each other.
  • The CJC/Ipa timing rule is the one to actually respect. Fasted, at least two hours after your last food. Carbs and insulin blunt the GH pulse this is designed to amplify. If you ate too close to bed, skip it rather than dose it — a blunted shot is a wasted shot.
  • Vivid dreams and heavier sleep in the first week are normal on CJC/Ipamorelin, and usually a sign it is working on the right window. Mild tingling in the hands is the other known one and it settles.
  • Take the IGF shot right after training, when blood flow to the worked tissue is highest. On a rest day inside a block, take it in the morning — do not skip a day to save it.
  • Do not extend an IGF block. Ten days, then off. The gap is the mechanism: continuous IGF-1 signalling is exactly the thing that stops working. There is powder left in the vial at the end of a block and that is expected.
  • Neither of these builds muscle on its own. They raise the ceiling on the training you do. If the sessions get skipped, both are wasted — which is why the training section of the Nutrition tab says change nothing about your program.
Note 05 — Missed Doses
Skip It, Do Not Stack It
  • Missed the Monday Retatrutide? Take it within 48 hours, then go back to Monday the following week. Past that, skip it and resume the next Monday at the same dose, not a higher one. Never double a Reta dose.
  • Missed a CJC/Ipamorelin? Skip it. It is a five-nights-a-week compound and one gap changes nothing. Do not take it in the morning to catch up — the whole point is the overnight window.
  • Missed a day inside an IGF-1 LR3 block? Continue the count the next day and finish the tenth dose one day later. Do not take two in a day.
  • Missed a MOTS-c? Take it whenever you remember, as long as it is before mid-afternoon. Later than that, skip it — the fasted morning window is the point.
  • Missed a whole week? Pick the schedule back up where you left it — the cycle simply runs a week longer and nothing about the sequence breaks.
Note 06 — Timeline
What Shows Up, And When
  • Wks 1–2 — appetite first. Reta's appetite effect lands on the first or second shot and is the earliest thing you will notice in the whole protocol. Deeper sleep from the CJC/Ipamorelin usually shows up within the first week of adding it.
  • Wks 3–8 — the scale, and the lifts. This is the window with the appetite compound in it, and where most of the loss happens. At 1–1.5 lb a week expect 8–12 lb by Week 8. The lifts holding while the weight comes down is the actual result — watch both.
  • Wks 3–4 and 7–8 — the IGF blocks. Better pumps and faster between-session recovery are what people notice first. Ten days is short by design; judge it on the training log, not on the mirror.
  • The weight keeps coming after Week 8. With the Retatrutide finished there is no appetite compound, so the rest of the 20 lb comes from the eating side alone — slower and lumpier. A flat couple of weeks in there is not a stall.
  • The cognitive block is same-day. Whenever you run it, Semax is usually noticeable inside 40 minutes of the first dose — the opposite of the bioregulators in every respect.
