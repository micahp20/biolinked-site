Your Stack, Explained
Tip 01 — The Most Important One
Perimenopause — What This Protocol Can and Cannot Do
You described low estrogen and progesterone across three panels covering about eight months. That is the single most important fact on this page, and it deserves a straight answer about what peptides can do about it.
  • Kisspeptin works upstream, not as replacement. It signals the hypothalamus to release GnRH, which drives LH and FSH, which drive your ovaries. If the ovaries can still respond, that is a real lever. If ovarian reserve is genuinely declining, no amount of upstream signalling replaces what is no longer being made.
  • A downward trend across three panels over eight months is a pattern, not noise. That is exactly the picture that warrants a proper perimenopause evaluation rather than self-management.
  • Ask for the full panel — estradiol, progesterone, FSH, LH, plus thyroid (TSH, free T3, free T4), DHEA-S, total and free testosterone, and SHBG. Timing matters: for cycling women these are usually drawn on day 3 of the cycle, and progesterone around day 21. See the Bloodwork tab.
  • HRT is the conversation to have. For symptomatic perimenopause with documented low estradiol and progesterone, hormone therapy is the evidence-based option, and starting it earlier in the window is generally more favourable. That is a decision for you and a provider — ideally one who treats perimenopause specifically.
  • These are not mutually exclusive. Nothing in this protocol conflicts with HRT. If you start hormone therapy, tell whoever prescribes it exactly what you are running here.
Said plainly
I am not a physician and cannot diagnose perimenopause or prescribe anything. What your own labs describe is a trend worth taking to someone who can. Book that appointment alongside Cycle 1, not after it — so that when you reassess in 6–8 weeks you have real numbers to compare against.

Tip 03 — Practical
Injection Routine & Site Rotation
No prior injection experience on file, so here is the whole routine. Two minutes once it is habit, and Cycle 1 is a single small shot a day.
  • Wipe the vial top and your skin with a fresh alcohol pad, every draw.
  • Pinch a small fold of abdominal fat a couple of inches from your navel, insert at 45–90°, push slowly, withdraw.
  • Rotate quadrants — upper-right, upper-left, lower-right, lower-left. Never the same spot two days running.
  • Push the big volumes slowly. Cerebrolysin at 30 units and FOXO4 at 60–80 both go in over 20–30 seconds.
  • Fresh syringe every time. A reused needle is a dull needle.
Penicillin allergy noted
None of these compounds are related to penicillin and there is no cross-reactivity concern. Mention it anyway to any provider you see about the hormone workup.
Tip 04 — What to Expect
Week by Week
A realistic timeline, so you know whether things are on track.
  • Cycle 1, weeks 1–2: quiet. Kisspeptin acts on an axis, not on a receptor you feel. PT-141 and Oxytocin are the only same-day effects, and only when you use them.
  • Cycle 1, weeks 4–8: this is the window where cycle regularity, mood steadiness or libido may shift. Re-panel at the 8-week mark rather than guessing.
  • Cycle 2, days 1–5: Semax is noticeable inside 40 minutes. Cortagen and Cerebrolysin feel like nothing yet — that is normal and expected.
  • Cycle 2, days 10–20: where Cerebrolysin shows up — recall, word-finding, mental stamina late in the day. Step Semax up at week 3.
  • Cycle 3: sleep is the marker. Yours is already 4/5, so watch for depth rather than duration.
  • Cycle 4: at 125 lbs expect 0.5–1 lb a week, not more. Faster than that at your size usually means muscle is going too.
