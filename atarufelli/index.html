Ashley's Four-Cycle Protocol
One goal per cycle, run back to back — built in four-week blocks, the length you asked for. Fat loss first, because that is the one you came here for.
In your words
You want to start seeing weight loss, more energy, and less brain fog. You listed fat loss as the primary goal, with cognitive enhancement, longevity and sexual health behind it. That order is exactly how this protocol is sequenced — the fat loss runs first and gets the most compounds, and nothing competes with it.
You have never injected before
That is the single most important fact on this page, and it shapes every number below. Every dose here opens at the low end of its range, the first cycle deliberately uses the gentlest possible on-ramp, and nothing starts on the same day as anything else. The needles are insulin syringes — shorter than a grain of rice and thinner than a hair — and they go into the fat layer under the skin, not into muscle. Week 1 has one job: learn the mechanics on two compounds. Everything else waits.
One goal per cycle
Cycle 1 — Fat Loss. Cycle 2 — Longevity & Anti-Aging. Cycle 3 — Sexual Health & Libido. Cycle 4 — Cognitive Enhancement, and that one is as-needed, which is how you asked for it. They run in that order with no overlap, so you never have more than four compounds going and you can always tell what is doing what.
Four-week blocks
You said you prefer four-week cycles, so the protocol is built out of them. Cycles 1, 2 and 3 are each two four-week blocks back to back — because almost every compound comes as a pair of vials (2 Retatrutide, 2 Cagrilintide, 2 NAD+, 2 GHK-Cu, 2 Kisspeptin, 2 PT-141, 2 Oxytocin) and that is one vial per four-week block, since a mixed vial only keeps about 30 days. Cycle 4 is a 20-day block plus a short tail, run whenever you want it. The Schedule tab has the week-by-week shape.
The 27 pounds

Expect to be 8 to 12 lb down by the end of Week 8 and to finish the rest across the later cycles on the eating side alone. That is not a slow result; it is the rate at which weight comes off as fat instead of as muscle.
Sedentary is fine to start
Nothing here requires the gym. The nutrition tab asks for walking and two short resistance sessions a week, added gradually — not because the compounds need it, but because it is what protects muscle while you lose 27 lb. Start with the walking.
Your PCP and your vitamin D
You are under a doctor's care and on vitamin D. Your July panel still reads 23.4, below the 30 floor, which means the current dose is not closing the gap — that is a conversation worth having at your next visit. Nothing in this protocol interacts with vitamin D.

Where the vial count and the four-week preference meet

You asked for four-week cycles, and that is what the vials are sized to — but a few of them are counted in weeks and a few are counted in doses, and they do not always agree. Three things worth knowing. One: a mixed vial keeps about 30 days refrigerated, which is what makes four weeks the natural block length in the first place. Two: almost everything comes as a pair — Retatrutide, Cagrilintide, NAD+, GHK-Cu, Kisspeptin, PT-141 and Oxytocin — and that is one vial per four-week block, which is why Cycles 1, 2 and 3 each run eight weeks rather than four. Nutrition
Fueling 172 → 145
The frame

Twenty-seven pounds is a real project, and the way to lose it is a modest deficit held for a long time — not a hard cut. Three things decide whether this works: a calorie target you can actually live at, protein high enough to keep the loss coming off as fat rather than muscle, and enough walking to protect the metabolic side. That is the entire plan. These targets run through all four cycles and do not change from Week 1 to Week 28 — what changes is how hard you have to work to hit them, and that is easiest while Retatrutide is running. Everything here is a starting point to tune against the weekly average, not a prescription.

Meal Structure
Three Meals + One Snack · Protein First
Anchor
A protein-forward breakfast, every day. It sets the day's protein total up to be hit rather than chased at 9pm, and it is the single habit that makes the rest of this easy. On the mornings you take Reta and MOTS-c fasted, that means waiting 30–45 minutes after the shot, then eating properly.
Protein first, then the vegetable, then the carb
Literally the order you eat the food on the plate. It blunts the glucose rise after the meal — which matters given the 104 — and it means you fill up on the thing you most need. This is free and it works at restaurants too.

Sits between lunch and dinner, the window where most days come apart. Decide what it is in advance. Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, a protein bar, jerky, an apple with nuts. Each of those is 20–25 g of protein and takes no cooking.

Nothing exotic and no food is off the list. Protein at every feeding, vegetables at two of them, most carbs unprocessed. That is the whole rule — the rest is portion size. A plan you will still be running in Week 20 beats a stricter one you abandon in Week 3.

How To Run It
Three Things That Decide The Outcome
The Real Lever
Protein Is The Only Thing You Have To Defend
  • If a day destroys the plan, hit the protein number and let the rest go. That single habit is what decides whether 27 lb comes off as fat or as fat and muscle together. Calories can be sloppy for a day; protein should not be.
  • Front-load it. 35–40 g at breakfast means you are already a third of the way there before the day has a chance to go sideways. Chasing 140 g starting at dinner does not work.
  • A shake is a legitimate meal on a Reta day. When appetite is genuinely gone, 40 g of protein in a glass is far better than skipping the feeding entirely.
  • Cheap high-protein staples: eggs and egg whites, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken thighs, canned tuna and salmon, lean ground turkey, frozen shrimp, whey. None of these require a recipe.
  • Weigh the protein for the first two weeks. Not forever — just long enough to learn what 5 oz actually looks like. Nearly everyone is 30–40% under when they guess.
Hydration & Energy
The Two Things That Read As Brain Fog
  • Under-eating and under-drinking both feel exactly like brain fog. On a GLP-1 you are at risk of doing both without noticing, because the compound removes the signal that would normally tell you.
  • Ninety to a hundred ounces, mostly between meals rather than during them. Drinking a lot with food makes the fullness worse on Reta days without helping hydration much.
  • Caffeine cutoff at noon. Caffeine has a 5–6 hour half-life, so a 3pm coffee still has a quarter of its dose working at bedtime. Your sleep is one of the best things on your intake — it is worth protecting.
  • Do not drop below about 1,400 calories. If the appetite suppression is so strong that eating feels like a chore, that is a reason to tier the Reta down, not to run a bigger deficit. Under-eating on a GLP-1 is the most common way this goes wrong.
  • Electrolytes on the low-appetite days. Eating less means taking in less sodium and potassium, and that is a real source of afternoon flatness. A pinch of salt in the water on those days is enough.
Adjusting
Tune It To The Weekly Average
  • Weigh 2–3 mornings a week, same conditions — after the bathroom, before food. Use the weekly average, never a single day. Daily weight swings 2–4 lb on water alone.
  • Flat average after two full weeks? Two options, in this order: add 1,500 steps a day, or drop about 150 calories from the carb side. Do not cut protein and do not cut fat to get there. Only then consider tiering the Reta up.
  • Losing faster than 2 lb a week? Add 150–200 calories back. Faster than that is water and muscle, not fat, and it comes straight back.
  • Expect the first week to look odd. A change in carb intake moves water weight several pounds in either direction before any fat has changed. The trend from Week 2 onward is the real signal.
  • Expect a flat month somewhere in Cycles 2 and 3. Those cycles have no appetite compound in them — the loss continues from the eating side alone, which is slower and lumpier. That is not a stall, and it is not a reason to add anything.
  • Re-set the calorie target around 155 lb. Maintenance falls as you do. A deficit that worked at 172 is close to maintenance at 155.

Notes & Tips
Running Your First Protocol
Note 01 — Your First Injection
You Have Never Done This Before — Start Here
  • Every shot on this page is subcutaneous — into the fat layer just under the skin, not into muscle. That is the easy kind. The needle is an insulin needle: shorter than a grain of rice and thinner than a hair, and most people describe the first one as anticlimactic.
  • Abdomen is the easiest site. Two inches out from the navel, anywhere around the ring. Outer thigh and the back of the upper arm also work once you are comfortable.
  • Pinch, insert at 45–90°, push slowly, withdraw. That is the whole technique. Pinching a fold of skin makes it obvious you are in the fat layer and not deeper.
  • Do the first one on a Monday morning with time to spare, not on a day you are rushing out the door. Lay everything out first: vial, syringe, two alcohol pads, sharps container.
  • Rotate. Move an inch or two from the last spot each time and work your way around the ring. Repeating the same square inch is what causes lumps and tenderness.
  • A small bruise or a red dot is normal and fades in a day or two. Injecting cold liquid straight from the fridge stings more — let the syringe sit out for a minute first.
  • Air bubbles are not a danger subcutaneously at these volumes, but tap them up and push them out anyway — a bubble displaces liquid, so it costs you dose accuracy.
  • Batch them, do not spread them out. On a three-shot morning, draw and give them back to back in one sitting — different spots, same five minutes. Trying to remember three separate times a day is how routines fall apart.
Note 02 — The On-Ramp
Nothing Starts On The Same Day
  • Never start more than two new compounds in the same week. This is the rule that makes a four-cycle protocol worth running — if everything starts at once you learn nothing about any of it.
  • Wk 1 — Cycle 1. Start MOTS-c and Retatrutide only. Reta is the compound with a real side-effect curve, so give it a clean week to be judged in.
  • Wk 2 — add the other two. NAD+ on the Monday, Cagrilintide on the Friday. NAD+ goes second-to-last on purpose — it is the one with a physical sensation, and you want to know it is the NAD+ you are feeling and not the Reta.
  • Wk 9 — Cycle 2. Start GHK-Cu and the Epitalon/Pinealon block together, and take the FOXO4-DRI pulse that same week. The block has to run consecutively from night one, so this cycle is the exception — but all of it is gentle.
  • Wk 17 — Cycle 3. Start Kisspeptin and Oxytocin; add PT-141 in Wk 19. PT-141 is the only one here with an acute effect, so it goes in once the quiet ones are established. All three swap to a second vial on the Monday of Wk 21, which is the only thing that happens in the back half of the cycle.
  • Cycle 4, whenever you run it. Start the Semax Amidate and Cortagen together; add the Selank Amidate the following week.
Note 03 — Retatrutide
The Compound That Does The Most And Asks The Most
  • Mild nausea in the first 24–48 hours after a dose is the known profile, especially in the first two weeks. It settles as tolerance builds. Lighter, lower-fat meals on Monday and Tuesday make a real difference; fat is the macro that makes it worse.
  • Three small doses a week instead of one big one is specifically to keep this manageable. Most of what people quit GLP-1s over comes from the weekly peak, not from the drug itself.
  • You drive the dose. Tier up only if the scale has stalled and the appetite effect has faded. Tier down — 0.75 mg (7.5u) or 0.5 mg (5u) — if nausea persists or you genuinely cannot eat. Neither direction is a failure; tell Micah which way you went.
  • Constipation is the other common one and it is mostly a water and fiber problem. The 90–100 oz target and the vegetables in the Nutrition tab are the fix.
  • Under-eating is the real risk, not overeating. If food has become a chore, that is the signal to tier down — not to enjoy the smaller deficit. Losing 27 lb slowly with your muscle intact beats losing it fast without.
  • Tell your PCP you are on a GLP-1-class compound. You are under their care and it is relevant to anything else they might prescribe — particularly anything that affects blood sugar or gastric emptying, and to any procedure requiring sedation.
Note 04 — NAD+
The One You Will Feel Going In
  • Flushing, warmth, chest or gut pressure, sometimes an urge to get up and move. That is the NAD+ and it is normal. It is dose- and speed-dependent, not dangerous, and it passes within a few minutes of finishing the injection.
  • Thirty to sixty seconds on the plunger. This is the single biggest lever you have. Pushing a NAD+ dose fast is what turns a mild warm wave into a genuinely unpleasant few minutes. Slow is the whole technique.
  • You are starting at 50 mg (25u), not 100. That is half what most stacks open with, chosen because this is your first protocol. Tolerance builds — by the third or fourth dose most people barely notice it.
  • Only step up if you want to. If 50 mg is completely comfortable after two weeks, 75 mg is 37.5u. There is no requirement to move; 50 mg three times a week is a real dose.
  • Do it last in the morning block. Reta and MOTS-c are quick. Give the NAD+ its own slow minute at the end rather than rushing it because you are already two shots in.
Note 05 — Bioregulators & Senolytics
The Ones With Nothing To Feel
  • Three of your compounds are Khavinson-class peptide bioregulators — Epitalon and Pinealon in Cycle 2, Cortagen in Cycle 4. They work at the gene-expression level on one specific tissue: pineal, neural and cortical respectively.
  • There is nothing to feel on the day, and that is not a failure. This is the most common reason people abandon a bioregulator early. They are judged weeks after the course finishes, by whether a baseline moved — not by an effect you can point at on a Tuesday.
  • Run them as blocks, not as habits. Epitalon and Pinealon are a 20-night course that stops dead on night 20; Cortagen is a 20-day course that stops on day 20. Do not extend either because powder is left in a vial — the consecutive-dosing block is the mechanism.
  • FOXO4-DRI is the same logic, compressed further. Three doses, then done. Senolytics are hit-and-run by design: you clear a population of senescent cells and stop. Mild fatigue or a flu-ish feeling in the 24 hours after each dose is the known profile and settles on its own.
  • They are among the cheapest parts of the protocol and the slowest to pay off. That combination is exactly why they get dropped, and exactly why they are worth keeping.
Note 06 — Missed Doses
Skip It, Do Not Stack It
  • Missed an AM shot? Take it whenever you remember, as long as it is before mid-afternoon. Later than that, skip it — the fasted ones lose the point, and Semax late in the day can push bedtime back.
  • Missed a Retatrutide? Take it within 24 hours, then go back to the normal Mon/Wed/Fri rhythm. Past that, skip it and resume at the next scheduled morning at the same dose, not a higher one. Never double a Reta dose.
  • Missed the Friday Cagrilintide? Saturday morning still works — it is a 24–48 hour effect, so it will still cover most of the weekend. Missed the whole weekend? Skip it and go again the next Friday.
  • Missed a night of a 20-night block? Epitalon, Pinealon and Cortagen all work the same way — continue the count the next day and finish day 20 one day later. Do not take two in a day to catch up.
  • Missed a NAD+, MOTS-c or GHK-Cu? Skip it. Never double a NAD+ dose — that is exactly how a manageable flush becomes an unpleasant one.
  • Missed a Kisspeptin, Oxytocin or PT-141? Skip it. None of the three has a loading effect that one gap undoes.
  • Missed a whole week? Pick the schedule back up where you left it — that cycle simply runs a week longer, and everything after it shifts by a week. Nothing about the sequence breaks.
Note 07 — Timeline
What Shows Up, And When
  • Wks 1–2 — appetite first. Reta's appetite effect lands on the first or second shot and is the earliest thing you will notice in the whole protocol. Expect the NAD+ warm flush on its first few doses; it fades.
  • Wks 3–8 — the scale. This is the window with an appetite compound in it, and where most of the early loss happens. At 1–1.5 lb a week expect 8–12 lb by Week 8.
  • Wks 3–8 — energy and fog, slowly. MOTS-c and NAD+ do not arrive as a lift you can point at. What people describe is afternoons that stop collapsing — you tend to notice it in retrospect, around Week 4 or 5.
  • Wks 9–16 — the quiet cycle. Almost nothing announces itself. GHK-Cu's skin and hair effect is the exception and usually shows around Weeks 12–14. The bioregulator read comes after the block, not during it.
  • Wks 17–24 — slow, then acute. Kisspeptin and Oxytocin accumulate, and this cycle now runs eight weeks rather than four, which is a much fairer test of them; judge them at Week 24. PT-141 is the opposite — loudest on the earliest doses.
  • The weight keeps coming after Cycle 1. Cycles 2 and 3 have no appetite compound in them, so the loss continues from the eating side alone — slower and lumpier. A flat month in there is not a stall.
