Ashley's Fat Loss Protocol
Four compounds, eight weeks, one goal — 172 lb toward 145. Three morning shots a week and one Friday evening.
In your words
You want to start seeing weight loss, more energy, and less brain fog. All three of those are what this cycle is built for — Retatrutide and Cagrilintide handle the appetite side, MOTS-c and NAD+ handle the energy and the fog.
You went with Cycle 1 on its own
The original proposal had four cycles. You decided to run the fat-loss cycle only, and this page now reflects that. The four compounds below are your whole protocol — same doses, same recon, same cadence they were built with. The three cycles you declined are archived at the bottom of this tab, clearly marked not taken, so the original reasoning and dosing stay on record without cluttering what you actually run. Nothing you declined is billed.
You have never injected before
That is the single most important fact on this page, and it shapes every number below. Every dose here opens at the low end of its range, and nothing starts on the same day as anything else. The needles are insulin syringes — shorter than a grain of rice and thinner than a hair — and they go into the fat layer under the skin, not into muscle. Week 1 has one job: learn the mechanics on two compounds. NAD+ and Cagrilintide wait until Week 2.
Eight weeks, as two 4-week blocks
You said you prefer four-week cycles, and that is how this is built. Three of the four compounds come as a pair of vials — 2 Retatrutide, 2 Cagrilintide, 2 NAD+ — and that is one vial per four-week block, because a mixed vial only keeps about 30 days. The swap lands on the Monday of Week 5. MOTS-c is the single vial and finishes early, around Week 6.
The 27 pounds
172 to 145. At the pace this is built for — 1 to 1.5 lb a week — that is roughly 18 to 27 weeks, which is longer than the eight weeks of compounds. Expect to be 8 to 12 lb down by the end of Week 8 and to carry the rest on the eating and walking side. That is not a slow result; it is the rate at which weight comes off as fat instead of muscle.
Sedentary is fine to start
Nothing here requires the gym. The Nutrition tab asks for walking and two short resistance sessions a week, added gradually — not because the compounds need it, but because it is what protects muscle while you lose 27 lb. Start with the walking.
Your PCP and your vitamin D
You are under a doctor's care and on vitamin D. Your July panel still reads 23.4, below the 30 floor, which means the current dose is not closing the gap — that is a conversation worth having at your next visit. Nothing in this protocol interacts with vitamin D.

How the four fit together

Retatrutide creates the deficit from a single Monday shot and is the compound doing most of the work. Cagrilintide holds that deficit across the weekend from a completely different appetite pathway, so it adds control without adding GI load. Four compounds, and the busiest morning is three shots plus one Friday evening.

The frame

Twenty-seven pounds is a real project, and the way to lose it is a modest deficit held for a long time — not a hard cut. Three things decide whether this works: a calorie target you can actually live at, protein high enough to keep the loss coming off as fat rather than muscle, and enough walking to protect the metabolic side. That is the entire plan. These targets do not change across the eight weeks — what changes is how hard you have to work to hit them, and that is easiest while Retatrutide is running. They matter more, not less, after Week 8, because from there the eating side is carrying the rest of the 27 lb on its own. Everything here is a starting point to tune against the weekly average, not a prescription.

How To Run It
Three Things That Decide The Outcome
The Real Lever
Protein Is The Only Thing You Have To Defend
  • If a day destroys the plan, hit the protein number and let the rest go. That single habit is what decides whether 27 lb comes off as fat or as fat and muscle together. Calories can be sloppy for a day; protein should not be.
  • Front-load it. 35–40 g at breakfast means you are already a third of the way there before the day has a chance to go sideways. Chasing 140 g starting at dinner does not work.
  • A shake is a legitimate meal on a Reta day. When appetite is genuinely gone, 40 g of protein in a glass is far better than skipping the feeding entirely.
  • Cheap high-protein staples: eggs and egg whites, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, chicken thighs, canned tuna and salmon, lean ground turkey, frozen shrimp, whey. None of these require a recipe.
  • Weigh the protein for the first two weeks. Not forever — just long enough to learn what 5 oz actually looks like. Nearly everyone is 30–40% under when they guess.
Hydration & Energy
The Two Things That Read As Brain Fog
  • Under-eating and under-drinking both feel exactly like brain fog. On a GLP-1 you are at risk of doing both without noticing, because the compound removes the signal that would normally tell you.
  • Ninety to a hundred ounces, mostly between meals rather than during them. Drinking a lot with food makes the fullness worse on Reta days without helping hydration much.
  • Caffeine cutoff at noon. Caffeine has a 5–6 hour half-life, so a 3pm coffee still has a quarter of its dose working at bedtime. Your sleep is one of the best things on your intake — it is worth protecting.
  • Do not drop below about 1,400 calories. If the appetite suppression is so strong that eating feels like a chore, that is a reason to tier the Reta down, not to run a bigger deficit. Under-eating on a GLP-1 is the most common way this goes wrong.
  • Electrolytes on the low-appetite days. Eating less means taking in less sodium and potassium, and that is a real source of afternoon flatness. A pinch of salt in the water on those days is enough.
Adjusting
Tune It To The Weekly Average
  • Weigh 2–3 mornings a week, same conditions — after the bathroom, before food. Use the weekly average, never a single day. Daily weight swings 2–4 lb on water alone.
  • Flat average after two full weeks? Two options, in this order: add 1,500 steps a day, or drop about 150 calories from the carb side. Do not cut protein and do not cut fat to get there. Only then consider tiering the Reta up.
  • Losing faster than 2 lb a week? Add 150–200 calories back. Faster than that is water and muscle, not fat, and it comes straight back.
  • Expect the first week to look odd. A change in carb intake moves water weight several pounds in either direction before any fat has changed. The trend from Week 2 onward is the real signal.
  • After Week 8 the pace slows, and that is expected. Once the appetite compounds finish, the loss continues from the eating and walking side alone — slower and lumpier. A flat couple of weeks in there is not a stall, and it is not a reason to add anything.
  • Re-set the calorie target around 155 lb. Maintenance falls as you do. A deficit that worked at 172 is close to maintenance at 155.

Reconstitution
Mixing, Step By Step
💧 First Time Mixing — Do It Slowly
The four volumes
Retatrutide 3 mL · Cagrilintide 2 mL · MOTS-c 3 mL · NAD+ 5 mL, each per vial — giving 10, 5, 13.33 and 200 mg/mL. That is 10u on the Reta (rising in 5u steps toward a 60u ceiling only if you tier up), 10u on the Cagri, 10u on the MOTS-c and 25u on the NAD+. Three of the four draws are the same mark, which is the whole reason this is an easy protocol to start on.
How
Wipe both stoppers with alcohol. Draw the water into a syringe, then let it run slowly down the inside wall of the vial — do not squirt it straight onto the powder. Swirl gently until clear. Never shake. Take your time on the first one; there is no rush and nothing is fragile except the peptide itself.
It looks like nothing
The powder is often just a faint film at the bottom of the vial — that is normal, not a short vial. Once mixed, all four go completely clear.

Storage & Handling
Keep It Cold · Keep It Clean
❄ Refrigerated, Protected, Labeled
Dry vials
Refrigerate at 36–46°F, protected from light. Never freeze. Unmixed powder keeps for a long time, which is why the three second-block vials simply wait until Week 5.
After mixing
Refrigerate and use within about 30 days. That 30-day window is what makes four weeks the block length here. Three of your vials hold more than 30 days of doses and get swapped on the calendar rather than on what is left in them: the Cagrilintide (4 doses used of 20), the NAD+ (about 13 of its 20 land inside 30 days) and the Retatrutide (4 weekly doses of 30 mg — 4 mg at the entry dose, up to 24 mg at the 60u ceiling). Fresh single-use syringe, wipe the stopper and your skin with alcohol, rotate injection sites. Three shots in one morning is the busiest it ever gets, and rotation matters from the first week. Work around the abdominal ring rather than reusing a favourite spot.
Notes & Tips
Running Your First Protocol
Note 01 — Your First Injection
You Have Never Done This Before — Start Here
  • Every shot on this page is subcutaneous — into the fat layer just under the skin, not into muscle. That is the easy kind. The needle is an insulin needle: shorter than a grain of rice and thinner than a hair, and most people describe the first one as anticlimactic.
  • Abdomen is the easiest site. Two inches out from the navel, anywhere around the ring. Outer thigh and the back of the upper arm also work once you are comfortable.
  • Pinch, insert at 45–90°, push slowly, withdraw. That is the whole technique. Pinching a fold of skin makes it obvious you are in the fat layer and not deeper.
  • Do the first one on a Monday morning with time to spare, not on a day you are rushing out the door. Lay everything out first: vial, syringe, two alcohol pads, sharps container.
  • Rotate. Move an inch or two from the last spot each time and work your way around the ring. Repeating the same square inch is what causes lumps and tenderness.
  • A small bruise or a red dot is normal and fades in a day or two. Injecting cold liquid straight from the fridge stings more — let the syringe sit out for a minute first.
  • Air bubbles are not a danger subcutaneously at these volumes, but tap them up and push them out anyway — a bubble displaces liquid, so it costs you dose accuracy.
  • Batch them, do not spread them out. On a three-shot morning, draw and give them back to back in one sitting — different spots, same five minutes. Trying to remember three separate times a day is how routines fall apart.
Note 02 — The On-Ramp
Nothing Starts On The Same Day
  • Never start more than two new compounds in the same week. If everything starts at once you learn nothing about any of it — and on a first protocol you want to be able to attribute whatever you feel.
  • Wk 1 — two compounds. Start MOTS-c and Retatrutide only. Reta is the one with a real side-effect curve, so give it a clean week to be judged in.
  • Wk 2 Monday — add NAD+. It goes third on purpose: it is the one with a physical sensation, and you want to know it is the NAD+ you are feeling and not the Reta.
  • Wk 2 Friday — add Cagrilintide. Its first dose lands on a Friday evening either way, so it slots in naturally at the end of the on-ramp week.
  • Wk 5 Monday — the vial swap, not a new compound. Retatrutide, NAD+ and Cagrilintide all move to their second vial. Same doses, same marks, fresh liquid.
Note 03 — Retatrutide
The Compound That Does The Most And Asks The Most
  • Mild nausea in the first 24–48 hours after a dose is the known profile, especially in the first two weeks. It settles as tolerance builds. Lighter, lower-fat meals on Monday and Tuesday make a real difference; fat is the macro that makes it worse.
  • One shot a week, Monday morning, fasted. The dose steps up 5 units at a time with a two-week hold at each step, and 60u (6 mg) is the ceiling — not a target.
  • You drive the dose. Step up 5u only if the scale has stalled and the appetite effect has faded. Tier down — 0.75 mg (7.5u) or 0.5 mg (5u) — if nausea persists or you genuinely cannot eat. Neither direction is a failure; tell Micah which way you went.
  • Constipation is the other common one and it is mostly a water and fiber problem. The 90–100 oz target and the vegetables in the Nutrition tab are the fix.
  • Under-eating is the real risk, not overeating. If food has become a chore, that is the signal to tier down — not to enjoy the smaller deficit. Losing 27 lb slowly with your muscle intact beats losing it fast without.
  • Tell your PCP you are on a GLP-1-class compound. You are under their care and it is relevant to anything else they might prescribe — particularly anything that affects blood sugar or gastric emptying, and to any procedure requiring sedation.
Note 04 — NAD+
The One You Will Feel Going In
  • Flushing, warmth, chest or gut pressure, sometimes an urge to get up and move. That is the NAD+ and it is normal. It is dose- and speed-dependent, not dangerous, and it passes within a few minutes of finishing the injection.
  • Thirty to sixty seconds on the plunger. This is the single biggest lever you have. Pushing a NAD+ dose fast is what turns a mild warm wave into a genuinely unpleasant few minutes. Slow is the whole technique.
  • You are starting at 50 mg (25u), not 100. That is half what most stacks open with, chosen because this is your first protocol. Tolerance builds — by the third or fourth dose most people barely notice it.
  • Only step up if you want to. If 50 mg is completely comfortable after two weeks, 75 mg is 37.5u. There is no requirement to move; 50 mg three times a week is a real dose.
  • Do it last in the morning block. Reta and MOTS-c are quick. Give the NAD+ its own slow minute at the end rather than rushing it because you are already two shots in.
Note 05 — Missed Doses
Skip It, Do Not Stack It
  • Missed an AM shot? Take it whenever you remember, as long as it is before mid-afternoon. Later than that, skip it — the fasted ones lose the point.
  • Missed the Monday Retatrutide? Take it within 48 hours — Tuesday or Wednesday morning is fine — then go back to Monday the following week. Past that, skip it and resume the next Monday at the same dose, not a higher one. Never double a Reta dose.
  • Missed the Friday Cagrilintide? Saturday morning still works — it is a 24–48 hour effect, so it will still cover most of the weekend. Missed the whole weekend? Skip it and go again the next Friday.
  • Missed a NAD+ or MOTS-c? Skip it. Never double a NAD+ dose — that is exactly how a manageable flush becomes an unpleasant one.
  • Missed a whole week? Pick the schedule back up where you left it. The protocol simply runs a week longer — but note that the 30-day mixed window does not pause, so a long gap may mean a vial gets swapped on the calendar with liquid still in it.
Note 06 — Timeline
What Shows Up, And When
  • Wks 1–2 — appetite first. Reta's appetite effect lands on the first or second shot and is the earliest thing you will notice. Expect the NAD+ warm flush on its first few doses; it fades.
  • Wks 3–8 — the scale. At 1–1.5 lb a week expect 8–12 lb by Week 8. This is the stretch with appetite compounds in it, so it is where the fastest progress happens.
  • Wks 3–6 — energy and fog, slowly. MOTS-c and NAD+ do not arrive as a lift you can point at. What people describe is afternoons that stop collapsing — you tend to notice it in retrospect, around Week 4 or 5.
  • Wk 8 — the re-draw. A fasting glucose, an HbA1c and a vitamin D are the three worth having. That is what tells you whether the 104 meant anything and whether the D supplement is working.
  • After Wk 8 — the eating side carries it. The compounds finish; the remaining 15–19 lb comes from the deficit and the walking. That stretch is slower and lumpier, and it is normal.
Note 07 — Your PCP

That is the clearest actionable finding on the whole panel and it is a straightforward conversation — the usual moves are a higher dose, taking it with a fat-containing meal, or checking absorption.

  • Nothing in this protocol interacts with vitamin D, and you listed no conditions and no allergies. The compound worth mentioning to any prescriber is the GLP-1-class one, and anything affecting blood sugar or gastric emptying.

What is worth adding or re-checking at the 8-week mark

Fasting glucose + HbA1c · the most valuable addition by a wide margin. The 104 was non-fasting and there is no A1c to read it against, so right now it is an ambiguous number rather than information. An A1c gives you a 3-month average; a fasting glucose gives you the number the reference range was actually written for. Both are inexpensive.

Vitamin D 25-OH · the fastest-moving marker on this panel and the one most likely to have changed. Re-check it first.

Fasting insulin · worth adding alongside the glucose. Insulin is what tells you whether the metabolic picture is about sensitivity or about intake, and it was not run this time.

Lipid panel · not ordered at all this time, which is why no cardiovascular domain is scored above. At 47 with 27 lb to lose, a baseline before the loss and a repeat after it is genuinely useful — weight loss tends to move LDL and triglycerides more than anything else on a panel.

Repeat CBC · to confirm the absolute lymphocyte count of 3.3 was the transient elevation it looks like.

hs-CRP · not run this time, which is why no inflammation domain is scored. It is inexpensive, and it is the marker most likely to improve visibly across a 27 lb loss — which makes it a satisfying one to have a baseline for.
