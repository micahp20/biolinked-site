  • Two hours clear of food, 30–60 minutes before bed, five weeknights. Food — and carbohydrate in particular — blunts the growth-hormone pulse this compound exists to amplify. A late dinner and a useful dose cannot both happen.
  • If you ate late, skip that night. A dose given on top of food is mostly wasted, and skipping one of forty is a rounding error. Do not push the shot to 1am to make the window.
  • This is the compound aimed at the belly fat, not at the scale. It preferentially moves visceral adipose tissue, so it changes how the waist measures at a given weight. Take the tape measure reading in Week 1 or you will have nothing to compare against.
  • Give it four weeks before judging it. The GH pulse effect is cumulative and overnight. Most people notice sleep depth first, somewhere in the second or third week, and the waist later.
  • Mild water retention, tingling in the hands, or joint stiffness in the first couple of weeks is the known GH-axis profile and typically settles. If it does not, that is a reason to drop to four nights a week rather than to stop.

Note 05 — Bioregulators & Senolytics
The Ones With Nothing To Feel
  • Three of your compounds are Khavinson-class peptide bioregulators — Epitalon, Pinealon and Cortagen, all three in Cycle 2. They work at the gene-expression level on one specific tissue: pineal, neural and cortical respectively.
  • There is nothing to feel on the day, and that is not a failure. This is the most common reason people abandon a bioregulator early. They are judged weeks after the course finishes, by whether a baseline moved — not by an effect you can point at on a Tuesday.
  • Run them as blocks, not as habits. Epitalon and Pinealon are a 20-night course that stops dead on night 20; Cortagen is a 20-day course running the same twenty days in the morning and stopping on day 20. Do not extend either because powder is left in a vial — the consecutive-dosing block is the mechanism.
  • FOXO4-DRI is the same logic, compressed further. Three doses, then done. Senolytics are hit-and-run by design: you clear a population of senescent cells and stop. Mild fatigue or a flu-ish feeling in the 24 hours after each dose is the known profile and settles on its own.
  • They are among the cheapest parts of the protocol and the slowest to pay off. That combination is exactly why they get dropped, and exactly why they are worth keeping.
Note 06 — Missed Doses
Skip It, Do Not Stack It
  • Missed the Monday Retatrutide? Take it within 24 hours, then go back to Mondays. Past that, skip it and resume the next Monday at the same rung. Never double a Reta dose.
  • Missed a Tesamorelin night? Skip it entirely — do not take it in the morning instead. The overnight GH pulse is the point, and a daytime dose does not do the same job.
  • Missed a KLOW morning? Skip it and resume the next weekday. It is a cumulative repair layer — one gap changes nothing, and there is no catching up to do.
  • Missed a 5-Amino-1MQ morning? Take it whenever you remember, as long as it is before mid-afternoon; later than that, skip it. It is a weekday compound and one gap changes nothing.
  • Missed a night of a 20-night block? Epitalon, Pinealon and Cortagen all work the same way — continue the count the next day and finish day 20 one day later. Do not take two in a day to catch up.
  • Missed a Kisspeptin, Oxytocin or PT-141? Skip it. None of the three has a loading effect that one gap undoes.
  • Missed a whole week? Pick the schedule back up where you left it — that cycle simply runs a week longer, and everything after it shifts by a week. Nothing about the sequence breaks. On the Retatrutide, resume at the rung you were on rather than the one the calendar says.
